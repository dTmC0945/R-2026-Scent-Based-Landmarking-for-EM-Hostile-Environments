1 Introduction

Electro-Magnetic (EM) wave communication has changed how information is exchanged and allowed communication of immeasurable distances to be measured in milliseconds. It also allows us to communicate with robots which can act in a wide variety of environments and has shown to be efficient and robust for most cases  [1]. However, not every environment can easily be navigated with EM-waves as caves and places which encountered natural disasters can prove hard to transmit information  [2]. The primary problem in using EM-waves is that they are attenuated as they pass through a conducting medium. There are numerous detriments caused by using EM-waves such as; range-dependent effects  [3], depolarisation effects  [4], antenna height gain effects  [4], the subterranean gallery geometry  [5], and multi-scattering effect  [6] on the propagating radio waves in caves.

An important application of interest is disaster scenarios  [7], where communication systems can fail and environment can become inhospitable for human exploration  [1, 8, 9]. While temporary infrastructure for communication can quickly be established, these can’t necessarily penetrate the rubble of collapsed buildings and can be generally the limiting factor of their applicability  [10] so algorithms for multi-robot exploration in these situations must be able to accommodate the limited and variable communication available to them  [11].

In a disaster scenario, It is necessary for the robots to complete exploration as quickly and efficiently as possible, and minimising repeated coverage of any area in the initial exploration phase helps reduce exploration time. With a small team of robots, it is common to use beacons to mark explored areas  [12, 13, 14], inspired by animal behaviour  [15], where these beacons enable the robots to reduce repeated coverage and explore larger environments without having to create maps.

Regarding a viable alternative to the aforementioned challenges is the use of scent based communication which has significant research behind it  [16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21] along with Molecular Communication (MC), where information is encoded into a chemical source  [22], which this research attempts to implement.

In this paper an alternative framework for mobile robotic communication is proposed for use in cave exploration/disaster scenarios for rapid exploration of the environment. To achieve this, scent-based packages will be used which will be placed by robots exploring, to provide simple transmission of information where these chemical messages will be designed using volatile organic compounds. To achieve proper data transmission, concepts developed in MCs will be used to analytically study the Bit-Error Rate (BER) and Mutual Information (MI) and its reliability of data acquisition.
